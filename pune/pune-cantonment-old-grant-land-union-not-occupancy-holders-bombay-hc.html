Pune Cantonment old-grant land belongs to the Union, not occupancy holders: Bombay HC

A possession decree against the Union of India in a case over 0.85 acre and a bungalow at Survey No. 742, Pune Cantonment, was set aside; the Bombay HC judgment itself orders no resumption or eviction.

Published 21 Nov 2026, 11:17 am IST · By Punevoice Desk

Share on WhatsApp

On 18 November 2026, Justice Gauri Godse of the Bombay HC held that the land at Survey No. 742, Pune Cantonment “was not privately owned and it was given on an old grant basis” (para 60). The court based this on the General Land Register (Class B-3) and the old-grant record.

Justice Godse partly allowed two second appeals filed in 1987 by the Union of India and the Military Estate Officer, Pune. The case, Union of India v. T. S. Anklesaria (a private trust), is over 0.85 acre and a bungalow at Survey No. 742, Pune Cantonment.

The trust claimed absolute ownership of the 0.85 acre and bungalow under a 1918 sale deed. Justice Godse found that the deed shows no source of title and that the Class B-3 entry was never challenged. The register entries, she held, raise “a strong presumption” for the Union (paras 53, 57–58, 79).

The judgment, quoting the Supreme Court’s 1999 Vakil ruling, traces an ‘old grant’ to colonial orders such as the Bengal Regulations of 1836. Houses could be built on cantonment ground, but “the ground continued to be the property of the State, was resumable at the pleasure of the Government”.

Resumption came on one month’s notice, with payment for authorised structures. Here, the General Land Register classes the land as Class B-3 and enters the trust as holder of occupancy rights, under a Government order of 6 January 1827 (para 54). That order, the court noted, confers no right of property in the ground.

Following the Supreme Court’s 2014 ruling in Dinshaw Shapoorji Anklesari, on similar facts, she held that the Union cannot be held a tenant and the trust has only a right in the structure (para 71). So there was no tenancy to end by a Transfer of Property Act notice (para 72). Separately, the trust’s 1980 notice was invalid because the suit was filed before the lease’s six-month notice period ran out (para 75).

The Rent Act does not apply (para 51). The trust prayed for no declaration of title, so it is not entitled to possession as prayed in this suit (para 79).

The decree for possession against the Union was set aside. The decree on arrears of rent, with set-off from the compensation paid with the resumption notice of 29 January 1972, stands (paras 81–82). Any right the trust has under the lease to seek possession of the bungalow is kept open (para 80). No costs.

As a High Court ruling, it binds the courts below it across Maharashtra and Goa.

For holders of Pune Cantonment’s old-grant bungalows, the ruling restates the courts’ view that the ground remains the Government’s and the holder’s right lies in the structure. The ruling lands on a long-running Pune dispute.

A February 2024 release by Rajendra Jagtap, Defence Estates Officer, Pune Circle, counts 202 old-grant bungalows or sites in Pune Cantonment. It says the land always belongs to the Government; a sale needs Government of India approval, and compensation on resumption is the construction cost alone.

In January 2024 the Defence Estates Office sealed four old-grant bungalows. It sealed one on Cross Road, the Director of Defence Estates said, on finding its sale unauthorised. In October 2025, Army authorities began the process to resume 12 bungalows.

The judgment itself orders no resumption or eviction. Whether the ruling will be appealed is not known.

Information: judgment of the Bombay High Court, 18 November 2026; Defence Estates Officer, Pune Circle.
