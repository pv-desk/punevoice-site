Phone hacks cost 11 Pune residents about ₹76 lakh in 15 days

A fake ‘APK’ file, once installed on a phone, lets fraudsters divert its calls and bank OTPs to another number.

Published 16 Nov 2026, 7:18 pm IST · By Punevoice Desk

Share on WhatsApp

Phone hacks have cost 11 Pune residents about ₹76 lakh in 15 days, with fraudsters hacking the phones and taking remote access to them.

The victims are from Swargate, Wagholi, Lohegaon, Kondhwa, Kothrud, the airport area and Bharati Vidyapeeth.

The bait is a message about an RTO e-challan, an unpaid gas bill or free movies, with a link on WhatsApp or SMS to a fake ‘APK’ file. Once it is installed, the fraudsters get access to the phone, divert its calls and bank OTPs to another number, and empty the bank accounts.

In Wagholi, a hacker took over the phone of a woman accountant at a company's office, deleted the number of a director's brother and saved his own number under that name. He then asked her on WhatsApp to transfer ₹16 lakh to a bank account, and she sent it, taking it as the brother's message. A case has been registered at Wagholi police station.

Other cases: ₹12 lakh through an APK file and ₹7.5 lakh through remote access in Lohegaon; ₹8.7 lakh taken from a woman's phone in the airport area; ₹6.81 lakh from a businessman through a fake e-challan app in Swargate; ₹6.25 lakh and ₹3.5 lakh in Bharati Vidyapeeth; ₹6 lakh from a senior citizen in Kondhwa.

Senior Police Inspector Swapnali Shinde of the Pune City Cyber Police Station has asked citizens not to click any unknown link received on WhatsApp or SMS, and especially not to download files with the ‘.apk’ extension.

Download apps only from the Play Store or App Store, check RTO challans and gas bills only on the official website or app, and if you suspect your phone is hacked, switch off the internet and change your bank and UPI passwords at once.
