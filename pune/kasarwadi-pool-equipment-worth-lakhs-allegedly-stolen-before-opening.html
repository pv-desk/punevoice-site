Kasarwadi swimming pool: equipment worth lakhs stolen days before opening

CCTV cameras, lights, showers and taps were among the items allegedly stolen from a Kasarwadi swimming pool days before its planned opening for citizens.

Published 16 Nov 2026, 7:56 pm IST · By Punevoice Desk

Share on WhatsApp

In a major theft, thieves allegedly cut through the metal security boundary of the Bharat Ratna Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Swimming Pool in Kasarwadi on November 9, days before the pool's planned opening for citizens, and stole equipment worth lakhs.

The items allegedly stolen include CCTV cameras, electrical wires, MCBs, lights, bathroom fittings, showers and taps. Two cricket bats from a nearby academy were also allegedly stolen.

Security guards were deployed in all three shifts, yet the theft came to light only on November 12. The incident has raised questions over the safety of municipal properties.

The PCMC Sports Department has assured further action, including a police complaint and an inquiry into the security arrangements.

Apte Road hotels: citizens ask why no action after FC Road crackdown

Some citizens are asking why hotels on nearby Apte Road, including Shreyas Hotel, Ramee Grand, Green Signal, Anna Idli and one more hotel, face no action after the FC Road encroachment crackdown.

The citizens allege that parking for all these hotels spills onto Apte Road and causes traffic congestion. They also ask whether hotels are even permitted in a residential zone.

Responses from the hotels and the civic authorities are awaited.

Laxmi Road · Civic

Massive Laxmi Road encroachment drive halted after Fatechand Ranka, traders intervene

A massive anti-encroachment drive on Pune's Laxmi Road was halted after the Laxmi Road Traders' Association and veteran trader leader Fatechand Ranka intervened, with civic teams pausing the crackdown.

Nearly all establishments on the commercial stretch pulled down their shutters during the drive, which tore down extended signboards, metal sheds and shopfront projections along the road.

Along the closed shops on both sides of the busy market street lay dismantled signage, torn banners and damaged storefront structures.
